What the options mean

How the fly learns

Bio-RL
The brain adjusts its own connections. Dopamine strengthens them after a hit and weakens them after a mistake.
Deep-RL
The brain stays fixed. A separate AI program learns which key to press from the brain's output.

How the brain is wired

Real wiring
Copied from a map of a real fruit fly's brain.
Shuffled wiring
The brain's overall structure (eyes to memory to output) is kept, but individual synapses are rewired at random. Tests whether exact connections matter.
Random network
Any neuron can connect to any other, so signals don't follow a real brain's route from the eyes, through memory, to output. Tests whether brain structure matters.
Dopamine blocked
Bio-RL with dopamine switched off. Tests whether dopamine drives the learning.

Deep-RL, real wiring, 150 songs

Deep-RL given 150 songs of practice instead of 30, to see if it just needed more time. It reached 75%, still below Bio-RL's 96%.

Each grey dot is the cell body of one of 139,662 neurons in a fly's brain and nerve cord (Janelia male CNS), seen from above with the head at the top. The empty band below the brain is the neck: only nerve fibres pass through it, so it has no cell bodies to draw. The simulated neurons come from the FlyWire brain and are drawn at a cell of the same type. When a note falls into a lane, that lane's visual neurons fire, then Kenyon cells, then the output neurons that choose a lane; each flashes as it spikes. For the Bio-RL fly, a hit releases reward dopamine and a mistake releases punishment dopamine, which strengthen or weaken the synapses that just fired.

Based on published research

  • Brain wiring and cell types: the FlyWire connectome of an adult fruit fly brain. Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024; Schlegel et al., Nature 2024.
  • Neuron model: Shiu et al., Nature 2024.
  • Brain and nerve cord drawing: the male fruit fly connectome from Janelia Research Campus and Google. Berg et al., bioRxiv 2025.
  • Dopamine learning rule: inspired by Bennett, Philippides and Nowotny, Nature Communications 2021.
